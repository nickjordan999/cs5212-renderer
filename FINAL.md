# Fresnel Caustics — FresnelRandomSpheres Preset

The `FresnelRandomSpheres` preset (in `src/ScenePresets.h`) composes four
distinct rendering ideas into a single scene: a Perlin-noise displaced water
surface, a collection of randomly-placed mirror and diffuse spheres half
submerged in that water, a forward photon-mapping pass that produces caustics
on the floor and on the diffuse spheres, and a path-traced diffuse shader that
gathers indirect light. This document describes some of the theory and the
implementation found in the source code.

## Scene layout

The preset is built around a Z-up coordinate system. A single point light sits
directly above the origin at `(0, 0, light_height)` with a separate
`photon_intensity` multiplier so the photon pass can be brightened independently
of the direct-illumination intensity (`Light.h`,
`ScenePresets.h:966-968`). A diffuse white floor lies at `z = floor_z` and is
tagged `is_caustic_receiver = true`. The water surface is a 2:1 rectangular
triangle mesh built on top of the floor; spheres are then sprinkled across the
floor with non-overlapping rejection sampling. Every fourth sphere is a perfect
mirror; the rest are randomly hued diffuse spheres flagged as caustic
receivers (`ScenePresets.h:1029-1086`).

## Fresnel equations

When a ray strikes the water surface — which is built from `Material::Water()`
with `ShaderType::DIELECTRIC`, `ior = 1.33`, and a slight cyan
`transmission` tint (`Material.h:54-60`) — the renderer splits illumination 
between a reflected and a refracted component. The split is governed
by the Fresnel equations. Solving the full unpolarised Fresnel formulae per
hit is expensive, so the renderer uses **Schlick's approximation**
(`Optics.h:27-40`):

```
R(θ) = R₀ + (1 − R₀)(1 − cosθ)⁵        with   R₀ = ((n₁ − n₂)/(n₁ + n₂))²
```


Refraction itself is implemented in `refract`
(`Optics.h:14-20`) using the vector form of Snell's law and returns `nullopt`
on total internal reflection.

In the dielectric branch of the shader (`Shader.cpp:170-191`), the shader
orients the geometric normal against the incident ray (using the
`is_front_face` flag set in the hit record), computes `kr` via Schlick,
recursively shades both the reflection and the (TIR-aware) refraction rays,
applies the material's `transmission` tint to the refracted radiance, and
returns `kr·R + (1−kr)·T`. Total internal reflection short-circuits to the
reflected colour alone, which is what produces the bright streaks visible from
below the water in mirror sphere reflections.

## Caustics via photon mapping

A regular path tracer rarely connects a diffuse surface point to a light
through a specific refractive path, so caustics under the water are noisy or
missing. The renderer therefore runs a **forward photon pre-pass** before
camera rays are traced (`raytracer.cpp:200-218`).

`Shader::buildCausticsMap` (`Shader.cpp:211-304`) emits `numPhotons` photons
from each light. Photons are sampled uniformly over the **lower hemisphere**
(`sampleLowerHemisphere`, `Shader.cpp:200-208`), since the light is above the
water. Each photon carries an initial power of
`light.photon_intensity · 2π / N`, the per-photon share of the hemispherical
flux. The photon then bounces up to `maxBounces` times:

* On a `DIELECTRIC` hit it computes `kr` with the same Schlick routine and
  performs **Russian-roulette splitting**: a uniform sample decides whether
  the photon reflects or refracts. This avoids the exponential branching that
  full splitting would cause. On refraction the photon's power is multiplied
  by the material `transmission` tint.
* On a `MIRROR` hit the photon reflects and is tinted by the mirror colour
  (here, white).
* On a diffuse hit, the photon **terminates** and deposits its current power
  into the caustics map only if it has gone through at least one specular
  bounce (`has_specular`).

The target is a `CausticsMap` (`CausticsMap.cpp`) — a 2D grid lying on
the floor's XY plane. Each cell accumulates vector flux.
Lookup at a shading point uses a **3×3 box kernel** divided by total kernel
area, then converted to Lambertian outgoing radiance via division by π
(`CausticsMap.cpp:46-68`):

```
L = Φ / (A · π)
```

An optional separable box blur (`CausticsMap.cpp:70-102`) further smooths the
photon noise before rendering. During shading the `DIFFUSE` and
`PATH_DIFFUSE` branches of `Shader::shadeRay` add `causticsMap->estimate(p) ·
material.color` on top of direct illumination whenever the surface is flagged
`is_caustic_receiver` (`Shader.cpp:147-168`). Because the diffuse spheres
poking through the water also carry that flag, dappled caustics show up on
their submerged sides as well as on the floor.

## Perlin-noise water surface

The water mesh is generated by sampling a classic **Ken Perlin gradient
noise** field (`PerlinNoise.h`). The constructor seeds an `mt19937`, shuffles
a length-256 permutation, and doubles it to 512 for wrap-around indexing.
`noise2D(x, y)` performs the standard four-corner gradient evaluation: hash
each corner into one of eight unit-circle gradients, take dot products with
the local offset, then bilinearly interpolate using the smoothstep-of-degree-5
fade `t³(t(6t − 15) + 10)` for C² continuity.

In the preset (`ScenePresets.h:982-1027`) the heights `h(x, y) = water_z +
amplitude · noise2D(f·x, f·y)` are sampled on an `(Nx+1)×(Ny+1)` grid, vertices
are constructed, and **per-vertex normals** are estimated from central
differences of the height field: `n = normalize(−∂h/∂x, −∂h/∂y, 1)`. Two
triangles per cell are emitted with these smooth normals, giving the water a
continuous Phong-interpolated normal across triangle edges. Without the
analytic normal the dielectric Fresnel branch would reflect/refract along
faceted triangle normals and produce visible terracing.

## Diffuse shading with path tracing

The non-mirror random spheres and the floor use diffuse materials. Direct
lighting at a hit point is computed by `computeDiffuse`
(`Shader.cpp:36-53`): a Lambertian `max(0, n · l)` term per light, occluded by
shadow rays when `shadows` is on, plus a small constant ambient.

For indirect light the shader supports a one-bounce path-traced extension,
`ShaderType::PATH_DIFFUSE` (`Shader.cpp:160-168`). At a diffuse hit it
computes direct lighting + caustics. Combined with anti-aliased ray batches per pixel (averaged in
`traceScene`, `Shader.cpp:100-112`), this yields unbiased, if noisy, indirect
illumination — visible as colour bleeding from one diffuse sphere to its
neighbours. The recursion is bounded by `maxDepth` so terminating rays
return the sky-gradient background defined on the scene.
